Tools of the Trade

Here are all the tools I utilize for creating art and games!

Most of the software listed is completely free and open source.

Traditional Art Materials

Watercolor Pan

This is just a simple water color pan I bought from Hobby Lobby. It is cheap and works really well. Watercolors are my favorite medium, as I find it intuitive to layer colors to make interesting textures.

Depending on how I feel, I may upgrade this to the Windsor and Newton version, as I feel like that would be a greater investment in the long run given the better paints and the fact that you can easily refill the pan with Windsor and Newston's watercolor tubes.

Ink Wells

As you likely guessed due to my love for watercolors, I have a love for inks as well! I primarily uilize inks for lining, however recently I began to utilize them to create pull pieces and have been having an absolute blast.

I currently have 4 types of ink at the moment: a simple black ink (from some random company), a blue ink, a white ink, and a red ink (all from Speedball). While the random black ink I found has certainly served me well and I would reccomend it as a good budget option, I'm afraid that nothing can beat the astounding* quality of Speedball inks.

*Disclaimer: I am not an expert of inks, please do not take my advice as gospel

Wacom Intuos 4

This drawing tablet was my first foray into digital art. I have had this tablet for around 8 or so years and it has never failed me. It is a fantastic little device and I plan to use it until it decides to break (or when the drivers stop working all together).

Huion GT 191

This is one of my most prized possesions. It still works incredibly well and can still produce amazing things. Even as a monitor it is still effective. It will be around 10 years old next year, so to celebrate I should probably get it a working pen...

Software

Krita

I have been using Krita for around 10 years or so and I don't believe that I have the need to switch to any other program (at the moment). For being completely free, Krita has pretty much every feature you need in order to create digital art/animations. Out of the box, there are several brushes to choose from (all of which are decent to great, in my opinion). Plus, you could always add more through the Krita Artists website.

I seriously cannot reccoment Krita enough for both artists and professionals. It is an absolutely fantastic program and has been a large part of my life.

Godot

Godot is the first game engine I began to seriously utilize/learn outside of university (for university, we utilized Unreal Engine 5). It is a fantastic, light-weight engine for creating both 2D and 3D games. In fact my first public game, Boberto Sanchez and Co., was made with the Godot engine. You can try it out through my Itch.io or through the link below!

Blender

A lot has been said about Blender that I cannot possibly sum up right now, however it is no secret that Blender is an excellent program. I'll discuss my two primary uses for Blender and my experiences with both of them.

The use that Blender is famous for, of course, is 3D modelling. I am not an expert at 3D modelling or animation (which is why I primarily work in 2D), however I cannot deny that using Blender for 3D modelling, while challenging to learn, is a pleasant experience. I have currently made a couple of models for the Tower Unite Steam Workshop (both being small objects). As for my other primary use of Blender, I use it to edit videos. For the videos I produce (namely showcases and animations), there is not much that needs to be done editing-wise, so Blender is the perfect tool to quickly cut together a video.

Bosca Ceoil

Bosca Ceoil is a simple program for making chiptune styled music. It is a nice and simple music app and I have made many songs with it (mostly for my own games).
